Have you ever walked into a lobby and just felt like it was expensive? You didn't see a price tag, but you just knew. That feeling is not an accident. Music actually influences what ends up in your client’s shopping cart. Without a single sign or a salesperson talking to clients, music becomes a hidden cue that raises their expectations and gets them to make elevated purchases.

Now, think about hearing fancy classical music like Vivaldi or Debussy playing overhead while you shop. Suddenly, the whole place feels more refined, and it gently nudges customers toward premium, high-end choices. This theory is a proven psychological trick.

<Figure src="/images/blog/music-and-customer-spending/image2.jpg" width={2048} height={1380} alt="A hand holding a bottle of red wine in front of a wine shelf" caption={"Classical music makes us reach for the top shelf.. Source: andresr / Getty Image"} />

In a famous 1993 study by Areni and Kim, researchers watched people shopping in a wine shop. When they played classical music, the shoppers chose significantly more expensive bottles of wine. The interesting part is that the shoppers didn't buy _more_ wine overall, they just bought the _pricier_ stuff. Why? Because as humans, we naturally associate classical music with top-tier quality, sophistication, and treating ourselves. The music subtly signaled that quality to their brains.

<Figure src="/images/blog/music-and-customer-spending/image1.jpg" width={2048} height={1365} alt="Supermarket aisle lined with wine bottles" caption={"Your environment secretly decides what you want to buy.. Source: Iaroslav Bushuev / Getty Images"} />

But music does not just make us spend more money; it can actually dictate exactly _which_ item we pick up. There is a wild experiment from 1999 by North, Hargreaves, and McKendrick that took place inside a regular supermarket. They set up a display with French wine and German wine right next to each other, making sure they looked completely identical. Then, they simply swapped the background music back and forth between French and German tunes.

<Figure src="/images/blog/music-and-customer-spending/image5.jpg" width={2048} height={1366} alt="Shopper pushing a trolley through a supermarket aisle" caption={"A simple change in background music completely shifts shopping habits.. Source: hiro2832 / Getty Image"} />

The results were completely mind-blowing. The alternating background music led to a complete shift in wine purchasing habits. On the days they played French music, the French wine completely crushed the German wine, outselling it five to one. But when they switched over to German music, the exact opposite happened—the German wine outsold the French wine two to one. It was a massive change in how people behaved.

Here is the most fascinating part of the entire experiment: very few shoppers consciously noticed the music was even playing. This proves that sound affects us deep beneath our conscious awareness. That is the true power of subconscious sonic influence at work. Even when we think we are making logical choices, the invisible soundscapes around us are quietly pulling the strings—influencing our bodies, our emotions, our thinking, and ultimately, our behavior and what we purchase.
